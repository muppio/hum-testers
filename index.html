MADE FOR THE EVERYDAY

For the words
worth getting out.

Some things come together when you say them out loud. Hum gives those thoughts a place in your messages, notes, and everyday work, on Mac and on Windows.

01 / YOUR VOICE

Your words. Your way.

Bring your names, specialist terms, and familiar phrases. Add snippets for the things you say often, and a writing style for each kind of app.

VocabularyParakeet ↗Your project name ↗
02 / YOUR COMPUTER

Recognition stays local.

Parakeet turns your recording into text on your computer. No cloud transcription service is needed for that step. The speech model is a separate download, about 670 MB, that you start yourself.

Your voice→Your computer
03 / YOUR CHOICE

A little polish. Still you.

Keep the recognized words, or choose optional AI cleanup. You decide whether its provider runs locally or online. If cleanup fails or takes too long, Hum uses your own words.

Know where your words go ↗
04 / YOUR APPS

It lands where you’re working.

Hum puts your words into the text field you started in. When it can’t confirm the field, it keeps them ready to paste instead of guessing. Sending is always a separate step.

Your words→The field you’re in
05 / YOUR THOUGHTS

Room for the longer one.

Build a draft over several takes. Keep notes in a notebook. Find what you said last week in a history you can search.

06 / YOUR RECORDINGS

Meetings and files, too.

Transcribe an audio file, or record a meeting from your microphone and your computer’s sound. Transcripts are saved on your computer, and a local AI model can turn them into notes.

All of this is in the Mac and Windows apps today, and both are still in testing. Windows is being checked feature by feature, and most of it is not confirmed there yet.

WHERE HUM IS HEADING

Stay with
the thought.

Hands-free, in development

We’re building toward an optional, fully hands-free Hum. A way to speak, refine, and get your words where they belong without reaching for the keyboard.

“Hey Hum” voice activation is in the app today, and stays off until you turn it on. We’re still refining how reliably it wakes, and it has not been tested on Windows yet.

    A few details.

    Can I download Hum today?+

    Not as a finished release. An unsigned pre-release for Windows is linked in the download section above, for people who want to test it. Mac is still in preparation. Each download shows its SHA-256 checksum and first-run steps.

    What can Hum do today?+

    Dictate into the app you’re using, with your own vocabulary, snippets and optional AI cleanup. Build a draft over several takes, keep notes, search your history, transcribe audio files, and record meetings from your microphone and your computer’s sound. These are in the Mac and Windows apps now. Both are still in testing, and on Windows most features have not been confirmed yet.

    Can I use Hum hands-free?+

    In part. You can turn on “Hey Hum” to start dictation, a draft or a meeting by voice. It is off by default, the listening happens on your computer, and it works best with a short pause after the phrase. We’re still improving how reliably it wakes, and it has not been tested on Windows yet. A broader, fully hands-free workflow is in development.

    Does my speech leave my computer?+

    Speech recognition runs locally using Parakeet. Optional AI cleanup is separate: it sends recognized text to the provider you configure, which may be local or online. Choose that provider according to your privacy needs. A private mode, built to deliver your words without saving the text or the recording, is in the app and still being verified.

    Will it work in my apps?+

    Hum inserts dictation into the text field you were working in, after checking it is still the same field. It leaves password fields and fields it can’t read alone, and keeps your words so you can paste them yourself. How well this works varies by app. On Mac it has been seen working in real apps. On Windows, only dictation into the Claude desktop app has been confirmed so far.

    Does Hum send messages for me?+

    Only if you ask it to. Recording, recognizing, inserting and sending are separate steps. Auto-send is an optional setting that starts off. It applies only to AI apps you choose, such as Claude, ChatGPT or Codex, and only when the message box was empty before you spoke.

    What’s different on Windows?+

    The Windows app shares its features with the Mac app, and an unsigned pre-release is available for testers. It is still in hands-on testing and most of its checklist is open. Speech recognition runs on the processor for now. A few Mac abilities are not there yet, such as delivering to a window that isn’t in front and voice commands that open or switch apps.

    What does “unsigned pre-release” mean?+

    Hum does not have code-signing certificates yet, so the Windows pre-release here is not code-signed. The one-line install command downloads and checks the file itself, so Windows doesn’t show a SmartScreen warning. If you download the file with your browser instead, SmartScreen will warn you before it opens; the download section lists the exact steps. Only continue if the file’s SHA-256 matches the one shown on this page.

    Where can I find the source code?+

    The source is not public yet. A source release is planned, but no license has been chosen, so there is nothing to link today. Open components and speech models keep their own licenses.
